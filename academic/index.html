About

I am an undergraduate in the Turing Class at Peking University's School of Electronics Engineering and Computer Science. I work on peer prediction with Prof. Yuqing Kong: designing mechanisms that make truthful reporting strictly optimal when information cannot be verified, and characterizing how much agents must communicate to achieve such guarantees.

Research Interests

  • Peer Prediction. Designing truthful, incentive-compatible mechanisms for information elicitation without verification, and characterizing the communication required for exact signal recovery and for strong truthful incentives.

Current work. We study two agents with a common joint prior, while the principal knows only a class of possible priors. For priors whose distinct signal posteriors are separated by at least d in Euclidean distance, we characterize the communication complexity of exact signal recovery as Θ(log N + log log(1/d)), and a one-round mechanism matches this bound. We further characterize the strongest uniform incentives achievable with payments in [0, 1]: the optimal loss per unit of transcript total variation is Θ(d2 + d/N). Finally, we show that even constant cooperative communication complexity need not imply low incentive-compatible communication complexity.
